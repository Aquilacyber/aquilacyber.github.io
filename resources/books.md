# Books

Start with the free ones.

## Free to read online

- [The Linux Command Line](https://linuxcommand.org/tlcl.php) by William Shotts. A complete introduction to the shell.
- [Automate the Boring Stuff with Python](https://automatetheboringstuff.com/) by Al Sweigart. A practical way into scripting.
- [Nmap Network Scanning](https://nmap.org/book/) by Gordon Lyon. The reference for the most-used scanner, from its author.
- [Crypto 101](https://www.crypto101.io/) by Laurens Van Houtven. An introduction to cryptography for programmers.
- [Security Engineering](https://www.cl.cam.ac.uk/~rja14/book.html) by Ross Anderson. A long book about why systems fail. Read chapters as you need them, not front to back.
- [Practical Networking](https://www.practicalnetworking.net/) by Practical Networking. Clear articles and diagrams on core networking.

## Paid, worth buying later

- *The Web Application Hacker's Handbook* by Dafydd Stuttard and Marcus Pinto. Older, but the method still holds. Use it with the PortSwigger labs.
- *Hacking: The Art of Exploitation* by Jon Erickson. For people heading toward exploit development.
- *Blue Team Handbook: SOC, SIEM, and Threat Hunting Use Cases* by Don Murdoch. For people heading toward security operations.

## For GRC and privacy

Read the primary documents first. They are free and they are what auditors use.

- [NIST Cybersecurity Framework 2.0](https://www.nist.gov/cyberframework)
- [CIS Controls](https://www.cisecurity.org/controls)
- The Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023 and the NDPC directive at [ndpc.gov.ng](https://ndpc.gov.ng/)
