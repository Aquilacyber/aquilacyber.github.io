# Introduction to GRC

Last reviewed: 2026-10-03

GRC stands for governance, risk and compliance. It is the part of security that decides what must be protected, sets the rules, measures the risk and proves to others that the rules are followed. It also covers audit and privacy. Where other domains work with systems, GRC works with decisions, policy and evidence.

## Why it matters

Technical controls are only useful if they are the right ones, in the right places and kept up. GRC makes sure the organisation chooses its protections on purpose and can show regulators, customers and auditors that it has done what it said. In Nigeria it matters more each year because of the Nigeria Data Protection Act, the NDPC directive and the CBN's cybersecurity framework. See the [Nigeria guide](../guides/nigeria.md).

## The three parts

- **Governance** is who decides. It covers leadership, roles, policies and how decisions are overseen.
- **Risk** is what could go wrong and what to do about it. You identify risks, rate them, choose a response and track it.
- **Compliance** is meeting the rules, whether they come from law, standards or contracts, and keeping proof that you did.

Two connected areas:

- **Audit** is an independent check that the controls exist and work.
- **Privacy** is the handling of personal data, which is law-driven and sits in GRC in most organisations.

## Key ideas

- **Policy, standard, procedure.** A policy says what is required and why. A standard sets a measurable requirement. A procedure gives the steps. See [frameworks and standards](../guides/frameworks-and-standards.md).
- **Controls.** Safeguards that reduce risk. They can be preventive, detective or corrective, and administrative, technical or physical.
- **Risk register.** A table of risks with likelihood, impact, owner and treatment. Use the [risk register template](../templates/risk-register-template.md).
- **Risk treatment.** Mitigate, transfer, accept or avoid.
- **Evidence.** If you cannot show that a control works, an auditor will treat it as not working.
- **Three lines.** A common model has operational managers who own risk, a risk and compliance function that oversees it, and internal audit that checks independently.
- **Third-party risk.** Suppliers and vendors who hold your data are part of your risk.
- **Frameworks.** NIST CSF, ISO/IEC 27001, CIS Controls, PCI DSS and SOC 2 give structure so you do not start from a blank page.

## What people in this field do

- A compliance analyst maps controls to requirements, collects evidence and supports audits.
- An IT auditor tests whether controls work and reports gaps.
- A data privacy officer advises on data protection law and handles breaches and requests.
- A risk analyst maintains the risk register and works with owners on treatment.
- A CISO leads the whole programme and reports to senior management.

## Common tools

| Tool | What it does |
|---|---|
| Spreadsheets | Risk registers, control lists and evidence trackers. The most-used tool in GRC |
| GRC platforms | Examples are ServiceNow GRC, Archer, Vanta and Drata. They track controls and evidence |
| Ticketing systems | Track remediation until it closes |
| Survey and questionnaire tools | Vendor assessments |

## Try it

1. Build a risk register for an invented company with the [template](../templates/risk-register-template.md). Score at least ten risks and write why you chose each score.
2. Fill in the [NDPA gap checklist](../templates/ndpa-gap-checklist.md) for an invented small business and write the top five actions.
3. Write a one-page information security policy for that business, in plain language.
4. Pick five controls from ISO/IEC 27001 and five from the CIS Controls. Find where they overlap.
5. List the evidence an auditor would ask for to prove that new staff are given access properly and that leavers lose it.

## Mistakes beginners make

- Confusing compliance with security. You can pass an audit and still be breached
- Treating a checklist as the goal and not the reason behind each item
- Writing policies nobody reads or follows
- Keeping no evidence, then being unable to prove a control worked
- Scoring every risk as high, which makes the register useless
