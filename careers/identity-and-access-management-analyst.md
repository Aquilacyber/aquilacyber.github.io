# Identity and Access Management Analyst

Last reviewed: 2026-10-03

[All roles](README.md)

## Summary

Makes sure the right people have the right access to the right systems, and nobody else. An IAM analyst creates and removes accounts as people join and leave, processes access requests, runs access reviews, supports multi-factor authentication and single sign-on and investigates login and permission problems. It is among the most accessible ways into security for people with a help desk or system administration background.

## Hard Skills

- Active Directory and Microsoft Entra ID administration: users, groups, organisational units and Group Policy
- The joiner, mover and leaver process, and how it works with HR systems
- Multi-factor authentication and single sign-on: how they are set up and why they fail
- Identity governance tools. Examples are SailPoint, Microsoft Entra ID Governance and Saviynt
- Role-based access control: designing roles and checking who holds them
- Access reviews: collecting the access lists, getting managers to confirm them and removing what is not confirmed
- Privileged access management basics
- Reading authentication logs, such as the Windows logon events in the [Windows and Active Directory basics](../guides/windows-and-active-directory.md) guide
- Ticketing systems and clear record keeping

## Soft Skills

- Patience with users who are locked out and in a hurry
- Firmness when a request does not match policy
- Chasing managers politely for their review responses
- Accuracy. One wrong group membership can expose a payroll system

## Education

No specific degree. IT support and administration experience carries most of the weight.

## How to get there

1. Learn Active Directory by building a domain in your lab. See the [Windows and Active Directory basics](../guides/windows-and-active-directory.md).
2. Follow the [identity and access management track](../tracks/identity-and-access-management.md).
3. Publish the single sign-on lab and the access review project.
4. Apply to IT support, system administration and identity roles. Many organisations move people into IAM from help desk teams.

## Certifications

Microsoft's SC-300 Identity and Access Administrator is the common choice. CompTIA Security+ and ISC2 CC cover the basics. Vendor certifications from identity providers help once an employer uses their product. See the [certifications guide](../guides/certifications.md).

## Interview Questions

- A person has moved from finance to sales. What happens to their access?
- How would you run an access review for 500 staff and 40 applications?
- A user says they cannot log in with their code. How do you troubleshoot?
- What is separation of duties? Give an example.
- A manager wants to give an intern administrator rights to a system for convenience. What do you say?
