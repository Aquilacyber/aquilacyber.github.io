# Workflow Engineer

Last reviewed: 2026-10-03

[All roles](README.md)

## Summary

Designs and maintains how work moves through a security team: how alerts become tickets, who gets them, how fast they must respond, how cases are escalated and how lessons feed back into the process. Where an automation engineer writes the code, a workflow engineer designs the process and configures the tools that carry it.

This is not a standard job title. Employers use "security operations engineer", "SOC engineer", "security process engineer" or "case management administrator" for similar work. Read the responsibilities in an advert, not the title.

## Hard Skills

- Case and ticket management tools. Examples are Jira, ServiceNow and the open source TheHive
- Designing intake forms, queues, priorities and service level targets
- Mapping a process end to end and finding where it stalls
- Writing runbooks and playbooks that analysts can follow
- Dashboards and reporting: mean time to detect, mean time to respond, queue age and backlog
- Basic scripting and API use, enough to connect tools or ask an automation engineer for what you need
- Understanding of the incident response lifecycle

## Soft Skills

- Listening to the people who use the process every day
- Explaining a change and getting people to adopt it
- Patience with process change, which people resist even when it helps them
- Clear, structured writing

## Education

No specific degree. IT, business process or operations backgrounds all lead here.

## How to get there

1. Work in a SOC or IT service desk and pay attention to where tickets get stuck.
2. Draw the current process for one type of incident, such as a phishing report, from first alert to closure.
3. Redesign it, write the runbook and, in a lab case management tool, configure the queue and the escalation rules.
4. Put the process map, runbook and your reasoning in your portfolio.

## Certifications

No security-specific certification is standard. ITIL for service management and a vendor certification for the case management tool your employer uses are common. See the [certifications guide](../guides/certifications.md) for security options.

## Interview Questions

- Walk me through a process you improved. How did you measure the change?
- How would you set priorities for incoming security alerts?
- An escalation path is ignored by senior staff. What do you do?
- What belongs in a runbook and what does not?
- How do you tell whether a process is failing because of the design or because of the tool?
