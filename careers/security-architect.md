# Security Architect

Last reviewed: 2026-10-03

[All roles](README.md)

## Summary

Designs how an organisation's systems are protected as a whole. A security architect decides which controls go where, writes the standards and reference designs that engineering teams follow, reviews the design of new systems before they are built and makes sure the pieces fit together: identity, network, data, applications and cloud. The work is done mostly on paper, in diagrams and in meetings, before anything is built.

This is a senior role. Most architects have five to ten years of experience as engineers, analysts or administrators. It is not a first job, but it is worth knowing the path from the start.

## Hard Skills

- Designing secure systems: segmentation, identity, encryption, logging and recovery
- Threat modelling a new system and choosing controls that address the threats
- Security frameworks, used as design inputs. Examples are NIST CSF, ISO/IEC 27001, SABSA and the Zero Trust guidance in NIST SP 800-207
- Cloud architecture on at least one provider, and how its security services fit together
- Network and application architecture
- Reading and producing architecture diagrams and design documents
- Knowing enough of each specialism, such as identity, endpoint and network security, to challenge a design
- Cost and trade-offs: the best control is the one that the team will actually run

## Soft Skills

- Explaining a design to engineers and executives in different language
- Influence without authority, because teams choose whether to follow your guidance
- Saying no clearly and offering an alternative
- Writing well. A design document is the main output

## Education

No specific degree. Experience in at least two security or IT disciplines matters more.

## How to get there

1. Spend a few years in a hands-on role such as security engineer, cloud engineer, network engineer or SOC analyst.
2. Volunteer for design reviews. Ask to sit in when a new system's security is discussed.
3. Practise by writing short design documents for systems you know, such as a small company's network or a cloud application.
4. Learn a framework well enough to apply it. See [frameworks and standards](../guides/frameworks-and-standards.md).
5. Add a certification such as CISSP once you meet its experience requirement, or an architecture-focused one.

## Certifications

CISSP is the most commonly requested. SABSA is a security architecture framework with its own certifications. TOGAF suits enterprise architecture. Cloud architect certifications from AWS, Microsoft and Google help in cloud-heavy organisations. See the [certifications guide](../guides/certifications.md).

## Interview Questions

- Design the security for a new online payments application. Where do you start?
- How do you decide what to put in a reference architecture and what to leave to teams?
- A product team wants to launch next week and your review finds a serious gap. What do you do?
- How would you introduce zero trust in a company that has a flat network?
- How do you keep a security design from becoming out of date?
