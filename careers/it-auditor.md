# IT Auditor

Last reviewed: 2026-10-03

[All roles](README.md)

## Summary

Independently examines whether an organisation's IT controls are designed well and working. An IT auditor tests areas such as access management, change management, backups, system operations and security, compares what the organisation says it does with what the evidence shows, and reports gaps with recommendations. The auditor does not run the systems. That independence is the point of the role.

IT auditors work in audit and consulting firms, in the internal audit teams of banks and large companies, and in regulators. In Nigeria, many come from an accounting or audit background and add IT skills.

## Hard Skills

- IT general controls: user access, change management, backup and recovery, job scheduling and operations
- Audit method: planning, scoping, risk assessment, sampling, testing, workpapers and reporting
- Frameworks used as audit criteria. Examples are COBIT, ISO/IEC 27001 and, in banking, regulator requirements such as the CBN framework
- Reading system configurations, access lists and logs well enough to judge them
- Spreadsheets and data analysis, including filters, pivots and increasingly Python or SQL, to test whole populations instead of samples
- Writing a finding: condition, criteria, cause, effect and recommendation

## Soft Skills

- Professional scepticism. You check the evidence before you accept an answer
- Diplomacy. Your findings are about other people's work
- Clear, factual, unemotional writing
- Discipline about documentation, because your work has to stand up to review

## Education

An accounting, finance, information systems or computer science degree is common. In Nigeria, professional accounting qualifications such as ICAN and ACCA are common in audit firms. No single route is required.

## How to get there

1. Learn the basics of IT controls and one framework. Start with the [frameworks and standards guide](../guides/frameworks-and-standards.md).
2. Practise on a lab: choose a system you control, define five control objectives, collect evidence and write up findings in the format above.
3. Apply to audit and consulting firms, and to internal audit teams, for junior IT audit or IT risk roles.
4. Add CISA once you have the experience it requires.

## Certifications

CISA from ISACA is the best known IT audit certification and requires work experience, with some substitutions for education. ISO/IEC 27001 Lead Auditor is useful for management system audits. CIA from the Institute of Internal Auditors suits internal audit careers. See the [certifications guide](../guides/certifications.md).

## Interview Questions

- How would you audit user access to a financial system?
- What is the difference between a test of design and a test of operating effectiveness?
- You find that the same person can request and approve a change. How do you report it?
- How do you decide your sample size?
- What would you do if management disagrees with your finding?
