# Nigeria guide

Last reviewed: 2026-10-03

Why security skills are in demand in Nigeria, who sets the rules and what to do about the local constraints. The legal and regulatory facts below were checked in October 2026. Rules change, so read the primary sources linked on this page before you rely on any detail.

## Why demand is growing

Three sets of rules push organisations to hire security and compliance people.

**Data protection.** The Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023 was signed in June 2023 and created the Nigeria Data Protection Commission (NDPC) as the regulator. In March 2025 the NDPC issued the General Application and Implementation Directive (GAID), which took effect on 19 September 2025 and replaced the earlier Nigeria Data Protection Regulation. Organisations classed as data controllers or processors of major importance must register with the NDPC. The Act also expects them to appoint a Data Protection Officer. Source: [ndpc.gov.ng](https://ndpc.gov.ng/).

**Banking and payments.** On 31 May 2024 the Central Bank of Nigeria issued its Risk-Based Cybersecurity Framework and Guidelines for Deposit Money Banks and Payment Service Banks. It replaced the 2018 framework for those institutions and applies from 1 July 2024. It covers governance, risk management, third-party risk, vulnerability management and incident reporting. Press coverage of the framework describes a 24-hour window for reporting cyber incidents to the CBN, so check the text for the exact rule. Fintechs that are not banks should confirm which CBN rules apply to their licence category. Source: [cbn.gov.ng](https://www.cbn.gov.ng/).

**Cybercrime law.** The Cybercrimes (Prohibition, Prevention, etc.) Act 2015 was amended in February 2024. It matters to you as a learner because it defines unlawful access. See the [ethics and law guide](ethics-and-law.md).

Anyone who takes card payments also has to follow PCI DSS, a global standard set by the PCI Security Standards Council.

## Who hires

This is a general picture and not a list of vacancies.

- Banks and payment companies, which carry the heaviest regulation
- Fintechs and other startups, which often want one person to do several jobs
- Telecommunications companies
- Audit, consulting and managed security firms, which serve many clients
- Government agencies and regulators
- Organisations abroad that hire remotely

Entry-level openings in most markets are more common in security operations and in compliance than in penetration testing. Regulation in Nigeria adds weight to compliance and privacy work.

## Regulators and bodies to know

| Body | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| NDPC | Regulates personal data protection under the NDPA |
| CBN | Regulates banks and payment institutions, including their cybersecurity requirements |
| NITDA | The national IT development agency, which publishes guidelines for the IT sector |
| NCC | Regulates telecommunications |
| ngCERT | The national computer emergency response team, which publishes advisories and takes incident reports |

## Local constraints and what to do

- **Data cost.** Plan downloads. See the [lab setup guide](lab-setup.md).
- **Power.** Snapshot your VMs, save often, and consider a power bank or UPS.
- **Hardware.** An 8 GB machine is workable. The lab guide lists browser-based options if you have no laptop.
- **Exam fees.** Most international exams are priced in US dollars and exchange rates move, so check the current cost in Naira before you commit. Ask whether an employer, a university or a scholarship will pay. Do not buy an exam before you can do the work.
- **Visibility.** Employers cannot see your skills unless you show them. A public portfolio matters even more when you have no brand-name employer on your CV. See [phase 4](../roadmap/04-get-hired.md).
