# FAQ

## Do I need a degree?

Not to start learning, and not for every employer. Some employers and some countries' visa and government jobs do ask for one. A degree helps most when you are applying with no other evidence. A public portfolio helps when you have none. Check what the employers you want actually ask for.

## Do I need to know how to code?

You need basic scripting by the end of phase 1. Python is enough for most beginners. GRC roles need less code than red team roles, but even a GRC analyst benefits from reading a script and using spreadsheets well.

## I have no IT background. Can I still start?

Yes. Start with phase 1 and take the time it needs. Many people join security from other fields, including banking, law, teaching and customer service. GRC and privacy roles often value that background.

## How long will it take?

At 8 to 10 hours a week, plan on six to nine months to be ready to apply for entry-level roles. Some people are faster and some slower. Study time you can keep up matters more than speed.

## What if I do not have a laptop?

See the [lab setup guide](lab-setup.md). Phase 1 and a lot of phase 2 work from a phone or a shared computer.

## Which certification should I get first?

Do not choose before you have read the [certifications guide](certifications.md). For most beginners, a foundation exam such as ISC2 CC or Security+ is the usual first step, and only if employers you want mention it.

## Is penetration testing the best way in?

It is the best known and the hardest way in. Most beginners find it easier to get a first role in security operations or compliance, and move toward penetration testing later if they still want to.

## Can I earn money from bug bounties?

Some people do. Most beginners earn nothing for a long time, because common bugs in popular programmes have already been found. Treat bounties as practice and stay in scope.

## Is it legal to practise hacking?

It is legal on your own lab and on platforms that give you targets and say so. It is not legal against systems you do not have permission to test. Read the [ethics and law guide](ethics-and-law.md).

## What do I do when I get stuck?

Search the exact error message, read the documentation, then ask in the [community](community.md) with the details listed on that page.

## How do I stay motivated?

Keep a weekly time budget, finish one thing before you start another, write up what you solve and tell someone your goal. Plan for slow weeks and do not let one bad week end the habit.

## Where do I find the big lists of tools and resources?

In the [reference section](../reference/README.md). Those lists are for lookup. Use them after you have a question, not before.
