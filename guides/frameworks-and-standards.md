# Frameworks and standards

Last reviewed: 2026-10-03

Security frameworks give organisations a shared structure for deciding what to protect and how. You will meet these names in job descriptions, audits and interviews. This page tells you what each one is and when you need it. Read the original document for any detail you plan to rely on.

| Name | What it is | Who uses it |
|---|---|---|
| [NIST Cybersecurity Framework 2.0](https://www.nist.gov/cyberframework) | A voluntary framework with six functions: Govern, Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond and Recover | Organisations of any size that want a common language for security programmes |
| [ISO/IEC 27001](https://www.iso.org/standard/27001) | A certifiable standard for an information security management system. The 2022 edition lists 93 controls | Companies that must prove their security to customers or regulators |
| [CIS Controls](https://www.cisecurity.org/controls) | A prioritised list of practical safeguards. Version 8.1 has 18 controls | Teams that want a concrete starting list of what to do first |
| [CIS Benchmarks](https://www.cisecurity.org/cis-benchmarks) | Detailed hardening guides for operating systems, cloud platforms and applications | System administrators and auditors |
| [MITRE ATT&CK](https://attack.mitre.org/) | A catalogue of attacker tactics and techniques seen in real incidents | SOC analysts, threat hunters and red teams |
| [OWASP Top 10](https://owasp.org/www-project-top-ten/) | A ranked list of the most serious web application security risks. Check for the current edition | Developers and application testers |
| [PCI DSS](https://www.pcisecuritystandards.org/) | A standard for protecting payment card data | Any organisation that stores, processes or transmits card data |
| [SOC 2](https://www.aicpa-cima.com/) | An audit report on a service provider's controls, from the AICPA | Software and service companies selling to larger customers |
| NIST SP 800-61 | A guide to handling computer security incidents | Incident response teams |
| NIST SP 800-53 | A large catalogue of security and privacy controls | Government and large enterprises, mainly in the United States |

## Nigerian requirements

| Name | What it is |
|---|---|
| Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023 | The main data protection law. Regulated by the NDPC |
| NDPC General Application and Implementation Directive 2025 | Detailed rules that implement the Act. In effect since 19 September 2025 |
| CBN Risk-Based Cybersecurity Framework 2024 | Minimum cybersecurity requirements for deposit money banks and payment service banks |
| Cybercrimes (Prohibition, Prevention, etc.) Act 2015, as amended in 2024 | Criminal law covering unauthorised access, fraud and related offences |

## Policy, standard, procedure, guideline

These four words are used loosely. In a well-run organisation they mean different things.

- A **policy** states what the organisation requires and why. It is short and approved by senior management.
- A **standard** sets a specific, measurable requirement that supports a policy. For example, passwords must be at least 12 characters.
- A **procedure** gives step-by-step instructions for carrying out a task.
- A **guideline** is advice that is recommended but not mandatory.

## How to read a framework

Do not read one front to back. Read the structure first, then look up the controls that match something you already understand, such as access control or logging. Compare how two frameworks handle the same topic. That comparison is the exercise most GRC interviews are really testing.
