# The roadmap

Last reviewed: 2026-10-03

Four phases, in order. Each one builds on the one before it and ends with a checkpoint you can test yourself against.

| Phase | Page | Time at 8 to 10 hours a week |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | [Foundations](01-foundations.md) | 6 weeks |
| 2 | [Hands-on](02-hands-on.md) | 10 weeks |
| 3 | [Pick a track](03-pick-a-track.md) | 12 weeks |
| 4 | [Get hired](04-get-hired.md) | Ongoing |

## How to use it

1. Set a weekly time budget you can keep. Eight hours a week for six months beats thirty hours for two weeks.
2. Work on one resource at a time and finish it before you open another. Collecting courses feels like progress and is not.
3. Take notes in your own words. Keep them in a notebook or a GitHub repository. You will reuse them in phase 4.
4. Write up everything you solve in a lab, including the easy ones. A write-up is a few paragraphs: what the target was, what you tried, what worked and what you would do differently.
5. Test yourself against the checkpoint at the end of each phase. If you cannot do one of the items, stay in the phase.

## If you are not starting from zero

- **You work in IT support or networking.** Skim phase 1, do its checkpoint, then start phase 2.
- **You are a developer.** Do the networking and Linux parts of phase 1, then go to phase 2 and spend your time on the web application labs.
- **You come from banking, audit, law or operations and care about compliance or privacy.** Do phase 1 in full, read the [GRC track](../tracks/grc.md) and the [Nigeria guide](../guides/nigeria.md), and do only the parts of phase 2 that interest you.

## When you get stuck

Search the exact error message first. If that fails, ask in the AquilaCyber community and include the command you ran, the output you got and what you expected. A question that says "it does not work" gets no answers. A question with those three details usually gets one within a day.
