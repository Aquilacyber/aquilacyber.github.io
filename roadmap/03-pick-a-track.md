# Phase 3: Pick a track

Last reviewed: 2026-10-03

**Time:** about 12 weeks at 8 to 10 hours a week.

**Goal:** go deep in one area and finish two projects you can show to an employer.

By now you have tried attacking, defending and reading logs. Pick one track and stay in it for the full 12 weeks. You can switch later. Switching after week three because another track looks more exciting is how people end up with a dozen half-finished courses.

## The tracks

Three core tracks suit most beginners. Four specialist tracks are for people who already have a head start.

| Track | You spend your days | Typical first roles | Good fit if you |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Blue team](../tracks/blue-team.md) | Watching alerts, investigating incidents, tuning detections | SOC analyst, junior security analyst, incident response analyst | Like investigation, patterns and working in a team on shifts |
| [Red team](../tracks/red-team.md) | Testing applications and networks for weaknesses and writing reports | Junior penetration tester, application security analyst, bug bounty hunter | Like breaking things, reading documentation and writing clearly |
| [GRC](../tracks/grc.md) | Assessing risk, writing policy, preparing for audits, handling privacy requirements | GRC analyst, compliance analyst, IT auditor, data protection officer | Like structure, law and working with people outside the technical team |
| [Cloud security](../tracks/cloud-security.md) (specialist) | Securing AWS, Azure or Google Cloud accounts, reviewing configuration, scanning infrastructure code | Cloud security engineer, DevSecOps engineer, usually after a cloud, sysadmin or developer role | Already work with servers, cloud or code |
| [Application security](../tracks/application-security.md) (specialist) | Finding and preventing flaws in software, working with developers, adding checks to pipelines | Application security engineer, DevSecOps engineer, usually after a development role | Can code, or want to, and like fixing as much as breaking |
| [Identity and access management](../tracks/identity-and-access-management.md) (specialist) | Managing accounts, single sign-on, multi-factor authentication and access reviews | IAM analyst, then IAM engineer | Come from help desk or Windows administration and like process |
| [Digital forensics](../tracks/digital-forensics.md) (specialist) | Collecting and analysing evidence from computers and networks, writing exact reports | Digital forensic analyst, incident responder | Like detail, patience and documenting every step |

## How to choose

Answer these honestly.

1. In phase 2, which exercises did you finish without being told to? Follow that.
2. Do you prefer to find one hard problem or to keep a system under control? Finding problems points to red team. Keeping control points to blue team or GRC.
3. Would you rather write a technical report or a policy? Reports point to red team. Policy points to GRC.
4. What do local employers hire for? Read the [Nigeria guide](../guides/nigeria.md). Entry-level roles in most markets open more often in security operations and compliance than in penetration testing.

If you still cannot choose, take blue team. The skills transfer to every other track. Choose a specialist track only if you already lean that way. Otherwise finish blue team first and add one of them after.

## What to produce

Each track page lists two projects. Finish both, put them in your GitHub repository and write a README for each one that explains what you built, how to run it and what you learned.

## Checkpoint

You are ready for phase 4 when:

- [ ] You have finished two projects in your track.
- [ ] Each project has a README another person could follow.
- [ ] You can describe your track and why you chose it in two sentences.
- [ ] You have read the job descriptions for at least five entry-level roles in your track and know which skills you still lack.
