# GRC track

GRC stands for governance, risk and compliance. This track covers the work of deciding which security risks an organisation accepts, writing the rules that manage them, and proving to auditors and regulators that the rules are followed. It includes data protection and privacy.

## What the work looks like

A GRC analyst interviews system owners, collects evidence, maintains a risk register, writes and updates policies, prepares for audits and tracks fixes until they are closed. You work with technical teams, lawyers, auditors and managers. Clear writing and patience with process matter more than any tool.

In Nigeria this track is growing because of new rules. See the [Nigeria guide](../guides/nigeria.md) for the Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023, the NDPC directive that took effect in September 2025 and the Central Bank of Nigeria cybersecurity framework.

## Skills to build

- Risk assessment: identify assets, threats and vulnerabilities, rate likelihood and impact, decide what to do
- How security frameworks are structured and how controls map to them
- Policy, standard and procedure writing
- Evidence collection and audit preparation
- Data protection principles: lawful basis, data minimisation, data subject rights, breach notification
- Plain, precise writing

## Plan

### Weeks 1 to 4: frameworks and risk

- Read the [frameworks and standards guide](../guides/frameworks-and-standards.md).
- Read the [NIST Cybersecurity Framework 2.0](https://www.nist.gov/cyberframework) and the summary of the [CIS Controls](https://www.cisecurity.org/controls).
- Read an overview of [ISO/IEC 27001](https://www.iso.org/standard/27001). The standard itself is paid. The overviews and the structure of the Annex A controls are enough at this stage.
- Learn the steps of a basic risk assessment and practise on an example you invent.

### Weeks 5 to 8: Nigerian requirements

- Read the Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023 and the NDPC's directive. Start at [ndpc.gov.ng](https://ndpc.gov.ng/).
- Read the CBN risk-based cybersecurity framework for deposit money banks and payment service banks, 2024, on [cbn.gov.ng](https://www.cbn.gov.ng/). Fintechs that are not banks should check which CBN rules apply to their licence category.
- Read the [PCI Security Standards Council](https://www.pcisecuritystandards.org/) overview of PCI DSS, which applies to anyone handling card data.

### Weeks 9 to 12: practice

- Build a risk register in a spreadsheet for an imaginary company.
- Write a short information security policy and an incident response procedure.
- Map five controls from one framework to five from another and note where they overlap.

## Two projects

1. **Risk assessment for an invented fintech.** Describe a small company, list its ten most important assets, identify threats, score each risk and propose controls. Present it as a risk register and a two-page summary.
2. **NDPA gap assessment template.** Build a checklist a small business could use to see where it stands against the Act: data inventory, lawful basis for each use, privacy notice, data subject request handling, breach procedure, vendor contracts and the question of whether it must register with the NDPC. Say clearly that it is an educational tool and not legal advice.

## Certifications worth considering

See the [certifications guide](../guides/certifications.md). Common choices are ISC2 Certified in Cybersecurity, CompTIA Security+, an ISO/IEC 27001 Lead Implementer or Lead Auditor course, and later CISA, CISM or CRISC, which need work experience.
